# Outcome Reliability Failure Signals

## Public Playbook

This playbook is designed to help identify recurring signals that the post-sale system may be producing internal completion without reliable customer outcomes.

It is based on the **Customer Outcome Reliability Model**:

```text
Promise
   ↓
Delivery
   ↓
Adoption
   ↓
Outcome
```

The goal is not to diagnose an account from one symptom.

The goal is to recognize patterns worth investigating.

> **A signal is not a conclusion. It is a reason to look closer.**

---

## Signal 1: The Dashboard Is Green, but the Customer Is Not

### What It Looks Like

- health score is green
- usage appears acceptable
- support volume looks normal
- milestones are complete
- internal reporting shows no major risk

But:

- the customer sounds frustrated
- executive confidence is declining
- stakeholders are escalating outside the normal process
- the customer is questioning value
- renewal sentiment feels weaker than the score suggests

### What It May Indicate

- health logic is over-weighted toward internal activity
- customer sentiment is underrepresented
- the wrong stakeholders are being measured
- usage is being mistaken for value
- unresolved operational friction is not reflected in the dashboard

### Reliability Question

> **What evidence says the customer is healthy, and would the customer agree with it?**

---

## Signal 2: The Ticket Is Closed, but the Problem Is Not

### What It Looks Like

- Support resolved the documented issue
- the ticket is closed
- the SLA was met
- the internal queue is clear

But:

- the customer still cannot complete the workflow
- the same issue returns in a different form
- another team becomes involved immediately
- the customer keeps explaining the original problem

### What It May Indicate

- technical resolution did not produce operational resolution
- the support scope was narrower than the customer impact
- a downstream dependency remains unresolved
- teams are optimizing for queue completion instead of customer outcome

### Reliability Question

> **What changed for the customer after the ticket was closed?**

---

## Signal 3: Implementation Is Complete, but Adoption Never Starts

### What It Looks Like

- configuration is complete
- training occurred
- launch date was achieved
- implementation is marked complete

But:

- usage remains low
- users avoid the workflow
- customer-side ownership is weak
- support questions increase
- teams continue behaving as if implementation is still underway

### What It May Indicate

- go-live was treated as the finish line
- operational readiness was never validated
- training did not translate into workflow adoption
- customer ownership was unclear
- implementation handoff was procedural rather than operational

### Reliability Question

> **Was the customer launched, or was the customer actually ready to operate?**

---

## Signal 4: Usage Is High, but Value Is Hard to Explain

### What It Looks Like

- users log in
- activity is increasing
- feature engagement looks healthy
- adoption dashboards look positive

But:

- executives cannot describe the business impact
- renewal value conversations are difficult
- the customer cannot connect activity to their original objective
- expansion depends more on relationship than demonstrated result

### What It May Indicate

- usage has become a proxy for value
- success criteria were never clearly defined
- outcome evidence is not being collected
- executive stakeholders are disconnected from operational users

### Reliability Question

> **What customer outcome improved because this usage occurred?**

---

## Signal 5: One Account Is Consuming Six Teams

### What It Looks Like

The account repeatedly involves:

- Customer Success
- Support
- Product
- Engineering
- Implementation
- Operations
- leadership

Everyone is working.

Nobody is sure the situation is improving.

### What It May Indicate

- fragmented ownership
- unresolved root cause
- poor dependency visibility
- conflicting internal narratives
- repeated symptom treatment
- no shared operating picture

### Reliability Question

> **What is the one customer problem these teams are collectively trying to solve?**

If that answer is unclear, coordination itself has become part of the problem.

---

## Signal 6: Everyone Owns It, Which Means Nobody Owns It

### What It Looks Like

- several teams are “involved”
- many people are copied
- follow-up is frequent
- action items exist

But:

- next steps are vague
- deadlines shift
- work waits between functions
- the customer keeps asking who is responsible

### What It May Indicate

- participation is being mistaken for ownership
- responsibility sits between teams
- decision authority is unclear
- coordination depends on informal follow-up

### Reliability Question

> **Who owns the next move, and what exactly are they accountable for?**

---

## Signal 7: The Same Problem Keeps Coming Back

### What It Looks Like

- individual cases are repeatedly resolved
- teams respond quickly
- customers receive temporary relief

But:

- the same class of issue reappears
- similar escalations occur across accounts
- workarounds become permanent
- teams become better at handling the problem without reducing its frequency

### What It May Indicate

- symptoms are being treated without addressing the system
- recurring issues are not being aggregated
- root-cause ownership is missing
- operational learning is not feeding back into Product, Implementation, or process design

### Reliability Question

> **Are we resolving incidents, or reducing the conditions that create them?**

---

## Signal 8: The Customer Has to Re-Explain Their Story

### What It Looks Like

Every new handoff requires the customer to repeat:

- what they were promised
- what has already happened
- which issue matters most
- what another team already tried
- why the situation is urgent

### What It May Indicate

- poor internal knowledge transfer
- systems of record contain fragments rather than context
- customer history is not being synthesized
- handoffs optimize departmental workflow rather than relationship continuity

### Reliability Question

> **Can the next internal team understand the customer situation without asking the customer to reconstruct it?**

---

## Signal 9: Executive Escalation Is the First Time Everyone Aligns

### What It Looks Like

Once an executive becomes involved:

- ownership suddenly becomes clear
- teams meet together
- timelines become visible
- decisions happen faster
- context gets consolidated

### What It May Indicate

The organization already knows how to coordinate.

It simply lacks a reliable mechanism for doing so before executive pressure forces the behavior.

### Reliability Question

> **What operating mechanism was missing before the escalation created one?**

---

## Signal 10: More People Are Added, but the Same Problems Continue

### What It Looks Like

The response to recurring post-sale problems is:

- hire more CSMs
- add project managers
- expand Support
- create another specialist role
- increase meetings
- create more reporting

But the underlying failures remain.

### What It May Indicate

- capacity is being used to compensate for process weakness
- role boundaries are unclear
- operating complexity is growing faster than the system
- institutional knowledge depends on individuals
- recurring work has not been converted into repeatable process

### Reliability Question

> **Is this a capacity problem, or are people compensating for an unreliable operating model?**

---

## Signal 11: Value Is Discussed Mainly at Renewal

### What It Looks Like

Value realization becomes urgent when:

- renewal is approaching
- procurement asks for justification
- an executive sponsor questions spend
- expansion is being considered

Teams then scramble to reconstruct evidence from the previous year.

### What It May Indicate

- value was not defined early
- outcome evidence was not captured continuously
- customer success focused on activity and relationship without maintaining a business case
- operational teams and economic buyers are disconnected

### Reliability Question

> **Could we explain the value delivered today without preparing for a renewal meeting first?**

---

## Signal 12: AI Produces More Output, but Nobody Can Prove Improvement

### What It Looks Like

The organization has:

- AI summaries
- automated notes
- generated account briefs
- copilots
- workflow automations
- new dashboards

But nobody can clearly explain whether AI improved:

- decision quality
- response time
- risk detection
- customer outcomes
- team capacity
- operational consistency

### What It May Indicate

- automation was implemented without an outcome hypothesis
- output volume is being mistaken for productivity
- AI activity is measured more carefully than AI impact

### Reliability Question

> **What became measurably better because AI was introduced?**

## Pattern Recognition

A single signal may be situational.

Several signals appearing together usually deserve deeper investigation.

Examples:

### Pattern: Internal Completion / External Friction

- milestones complete
- tickets closed
- health score green
- customer still frustrated

This suggests internal systems may be measuring completion better than resolution.

### Pattern: Cross-Functional Drag

- many teams involved
- repeated escalation
- unclear ownership
- customer re-explains context

This suggests the handoffs between functions may be less reliable than the functions themselves.

### Pattern: Adoption Without Value

- implementation complete
- usage acceptable
- executive value unclear
- renewal justification difficult

This suggests the organization can produce activity but may not be systematically proving outcome.

### Pattern: Heroic Operations

- one person knows everything
- follow-up depends on memory
- recurring issues are solved manually
- adding people temporarily improves performance

This suggests the operating model may depend on heroics instead of repeatability.

---

## What To Do With a Signal

Publicly, the next step is deliberately simple:

1. **Name the mismatch.**
2. **Identify which stage or transition it affects.**
3. **Gather evidence from both internal systems and customer reality.**
4. **Clarify ownership.**
5. **Determine whether the issue is a symptom, dependency, or root cause.**
6. **Verify whether the eventual action changed the customer outcome.**

The deeper diagnostic and remediation method is intentionally not published here.

---

## What This Playbook Is Not

This is not:

- a churn model
- a health-score framework
- a prioritization algorithm
- an escalation SOP
- a remediation workflow
- an operating maturity model

It is a **pattern-recognition tool**.

Its job is to help teams notice when internal progress and customer reality may be telling different stories.

---

## Portfolio Boundary

The public playbook intentionally excludes:

- signal weighting
- severity scoring
- maturity criteria
- evidence thresholds
- account prioritization logic
- root-cause mapping methods
- remediation sequences
- executive intervention rules
- automation logic
- AI-assisted detection prompts

Those belong to the private implementation layer.

> **The public question is: “Where is reality diverging from the system?”**

> **The private work begins with deciding why.**
