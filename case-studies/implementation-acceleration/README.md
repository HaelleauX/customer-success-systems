# Implementation Acceleration

**Reduced typical implementation-to-go-live time from approximately 12 weeks to 6 weeks, a 50% reduction.**

*Professional case study | Generalized reconstruction*

## The Problem

Customer launches depended on stakeholder decisions, workflow alignment, training, and operational readiness. Inconsistent preparation and unclear ownership allowed dependencies to surface late, extending the implementation cycle to approximately 12 weeks.

## My Contribution

I standardized the implementation approach and coordinated customer and internal stakeholders around the conditions required for a successful launch.

- **Defined a repeatable path:** clearer stages from kickoff and discovery through training, readiness review, go-live, and handoff.
- **Moved readiness work earlier:** surfaced customer requirements, decisions, and workflow dependencies before they became launch blockers.
- **Made follow-through explicit:** clarified the owner, next action, and expected timing for open work.
- **Built risk review into delivery:** used missed milestones, unresolved questions, and stalled dependencies to guide intervention.
- **Aligned communication:** gave stakeholders a shared view of progress, responsibilities, and decisions needed.

## Result

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical implementation-to-go-live timeline | ~12 weeks | ~6 weeks |

The approximately 50% reduction reflects time to go-live. Go-live creates an opportunity for value; sustained adoption and customer outcomes require continued follow-through.

## The Judgment Behind the Work

The key question was: **What must be true for the next stage to succeed?**

That shifted attention toward readiness and dependencies, helping teams remove avoidable delay while preserving customer-specific needs.

**Capabilities demonstrated:** implementation strategy, process improvement, cross-functional delivery, stakeholder alignment, risk management, and operational handoffs.
