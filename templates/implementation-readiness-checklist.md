# Implementation Readiness Checklist

**Illustrative portfolio sample | Not an employer artifact | Not evidence of historical deployment**

This checklist is a generalized example of how implementation readiness can be made visible before a go-live decision. It reflects implementation and Customer Success principles demonstrated in this portfolio, but it is **not copied from any employer-owned checklist, internal template, or customer document**, and it should not be read as evidence that this exact artifact was used in a historical implementation.

The purpose is to show the operating logic behind launch readiness: define what must be true, identify who owns it, make dependencies visible, require evidence, surface blockers, and make the go-live decision explicit.

## Readiness Review

| Readiness area | Readiness criterion | Owner | Dependencies | Required evidence | Blocker if unmet? | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stakeholder alignment | Executive sponsor and day-to-day customer owner are identified; decision authority is clear | Customer Success / Customer lead | Contract handoff, stakeholder map | Named owners, confirmed responsibilities, escalation path | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Scope & expectations | Implementation scope, target workflow, launch objective, and material assumptions are understood by both sides | Implementation lead / Customer Success | Sales handoff, discovery | Confirmed scope summary, open-assumption log | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Workflow readiness | Future-state workflow has been reviewed with the people responsible for operating it | Customer operations owner | Discovery, configuration decisions | Approved workflow or documented operating process | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Technical / data readiness | Required integrations, data inputs, access, configuration, and technical dependencies are complete or explicitly accepted | Technical owner | Customer IT, vendor dependencies, access credentials | Test results, configuration confirmation, dependency status | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| User access | Required users or user groups can access the environment with appropriate permissions | Technical owner / Customer admin | Provisioning, identity/access setup | Access validation for required roles | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Training & enablement | Required audiences understand the launch workflow and know where to get support | Customer Success / Training owner | Final workflow, user access | Training completion, materials available, unanswered questions logged | Conditional | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Support & escalation | Post-launch support path, escalation route, and internal ownership are defined | Customer Success / Support lead | Handoff agreement | Documented support path and escalation contacts | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Open issues | Remaining issues have an owner, customer impact assessment, next action, and target date | Relevant functional owner | Cross-functional action | Open-issue log with ownership and timing | Depends on impact | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Success measurement | Initial adoption or value signals are defined so the team can verify what happens after launch | Customer Success / Customer sponsor | Customer objective, available data | Agreed post-launch indicators and review timing | No | Not started / At risk / Ready |
| Launch communication | Customer and internal stakeholders understand the launch date, responsibilities, known limitations, and next review point | Customer Success / Implementation lead | Readiness review | Final launch communication or meeting record | Yes | Not started / At risk / Ready |

## Blocker Review

For each unresolved item, document:

| Blocker / risk | Customer impact | Owner | Dependency | Required evidence to clear | Target date | Escalation needed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example: required access not provisioned | Users cannot complete launch workflow | Customer admin | Identity/access setup | Successful access validation | YYYY-MM-DD | Yes / No |

A blocker should not disappear because a meeting ended. It is cleared when the required condition is met and the evidence supports that conclusion.

## Go-Live Decision

**Decision:** GO / CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO

Before approving launch, confirm:

- required readiness criteria are met or consciously accepted as exceptions
- unresolved blockers and customer impact are visible
- each exception has a named owner and target date
- customer and internal stakeholders understand known limitations
- post-launch support and escalation ownership are active
- initial adoption or outcome checks are scheduled

### Decision Record

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed go-live date | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Decision | GO / CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO |
| Critical blockers remaining | None / List |
| Accepted exceptions | None / List |
| Exception owner(s) | Name / Role |
| Required follow-up date(s) | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Customer decision-maker | Name / Role |
| Internal decision-maker | Name / Role |
| First post-launch review | YYYY-MM-DD |
| Decision rationale | Brief evidence-based summary |

## Portfolio Note

This is an **illustrative portfolio sample**, created to demonstrate implementation-readiness thinking and document design. It is not presented as an original employer artifact, a customer deliverable, or proof that this exact checklist was deployed historically.

The verified professional result connected to the implementation case study is the reduction in typical implementation-to-go-live time from approximately **12 weeks to 6 weeks**. The case study itself is a generalized reconstruction; this checklist is a separate illustrative sample.
