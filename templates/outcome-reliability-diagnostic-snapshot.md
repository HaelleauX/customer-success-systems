# Outcome Reliability Diagnostic Snapshot

## Public Portfolio Tool

This lightweight diagnostic is designed to help identify where customer value may be breaking down across the post-sale journey.

It is based on the public **Customer Outcome Reliability Model**:

```text
Promise
   ↓
Delivery
   ↓
Adoption
   ↓
Outcome
```

The purpose is not to produce a score.

The purpose is to make misalignment visible.

> **If internal status and customer reality disagree, investigate the gap.**

---

## How to Use This Snapshot

Review each stage and ask whether the organization has enough evidence to answer the questions confidently.

The most useful signals are often not the individual answers.

They are the places where teams answer the same question differently.

---

## 1. Promise

### Core Question

> **What does the customer believe they bought?**

### Diagnostic Questions

- Is the expected customer outcome clearly defined?
- Do Sales, Implementation, Customer Success, and the customer describe that outcome the same way?
- Are timeline expectations explicit?
- Are major assumptions documented?
- Are known dependencies visible before handoff?
- Is there clarity about what is included, excluded, or conditional?

### Watch For

- different versions of the original commitment
- unstated assumptions becoming “requirements” later
- timelines that depend on customer actions nobody surfaced
- implementation discovering expectations that originated during Sales
- value language that is too vague to measure later

---

## 2. Delivery

### Core Question

> **Did we create the conditions required for the customer to succeed?**

### Diagnostic Questions

- Are implementation milestones tied to readiness, not just task completion?
- Are customer-side dependencies visible?
- Is ownership clear for unresolved work?
- Have workflow and operational requirements been validated?
- Are risks surfaced before they affect launch?
- Is handoff into the next phase explicit?

### Watch For

- “complete” milestones with unresolved prerequisites
- repeated follow-up because ownership is unclear
- late discovery of workflow or technical dependencies
- go-live dates treated as proof of readiness
- customer confusion about what happens next

---

## 3. Adoption

### Core Question

> **Is the customer actually able and willing to use what was delivered?**

### Diagnostic Questions

- Is the product embedded in real customer workflow?
- Do users understand how and when to use it?
- Is there clear internal ownership on the customer side?
- Are enablement and training connected to actual use cases?
- Are adoption barriers being diagnosed, not just reported?
- Does utilization data align with stakeholder feedback?

### Watch For

- technically live customers with little repeatable use
- training completed but workflow unchanged
- high support volume after implementation
- utilization reported without context
- stakeholder disengagement hidden behind activity metrics

---

## 4. Outcome

### Core Question

> **Can we demonstrate that the customer is receiving meaningful value?**

### Diagnostic Questions

- Are success criteria defined in customer-relevant terms?
- Is value evidence connected to the customer’s original objective?
- Can the customer describe the benefit in their own language?
- Are executive stakeholders seeing evidence that matters to them?
- Is value discussed before renewal?
- Are outcomes sustained, or dependent on one heroic employee?

### Watch For

- usage treated as proof of value
- health scores with no customer validation
- value conversations beginning only near renewal
- executive sponsors unable to articulate realized impact
- customer success measured mostly through internal activity

---

## Cross-Stage Reliability Signals

Some of the most important indicators appear when two signals disagree.

| Internal Signal | Customer-Reality Check |
| --- | --- |
| Milestone complete | Is the customer actually ready? |
| Ticket closed | Is the customer problem actually resolved? |
| Go-live achieved | Is the capability being used successfully? |
| Usage increased | Is the customer receiving value? |
| Health score green | Does the customer agree? |
| Owner assigned | Does everyone know who owns the next action? |
| Executive update sent | Did the update clarify the decision or risk? |

When those answers diverge, the gap deserves investigation.

---

## Quick Reflection

A post-sale system may need attention when several of the following are true:

- customers escalate issues that internal teams believed were resolved
- implementation completion does not lead to adoption
- adoption does not clearly connect to value
- the same customer problem crosses multiple departments
- internal teams disagree on ownership
- executive stakeholders see surprises late
- health reporting conflicts with customer sentiment
- customer-facing teams spend significant time reconstructing context
- recurring issues return after individual fixes
- value depends on informal heroics rather than repeatable systems

---

## What This Snapshot Is Not

This is not:

- a scoring model
- a maturity assessment
- a customer-health algorithm
- a churn predictor
- a consulting assessment
- a remediation playbook

Those require deeper context and judgment.

This public tool is intentionally limited to one job:

> **Help identify where the promise-to-outcome chain may be unreliable.**

---

## Portfolio Boundary

The full diagnostic methodology is intentionally not published.

Private elements may include:

- scoring logic
- maturity bands
- weighting rules
- interview guides
- evidence requirements
- facilitation materials
- prioritization models
- remediation pathways
- executive workshop formats
- AI-assisted analysis workflows

The public version demonstrates the diagnostic lens without publishing the full assessment engine.
